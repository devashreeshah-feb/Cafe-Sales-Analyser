1. Executive Summary
This document defines the Product Requirements and Machine Learning Model Specification for the Café Sales Analytics Platform — a full-stack intelligence system built to transform raw point-of-sale transaction data into actionable business insights for café operators.
The platform ingests cleaned transaction data (10,000 records, Jan–Dec 2023), applies a Random Forest regression model, and surfaces insights through a React-based dashboard covering revenue trends, profitability analysis, data quality audits, and forecasting.

2. Problem Statement
2.1 Business Context
Café operators generate large volumes of transaction data through point-of-sale systems, but lack the tooling to derive meaningful revenue intelligence from it. Manual Excel analysis is slow, error-prone, and produces static snapshots rather than living insights.
2.2 Key Pain Points
No visibility into which menu items are most profitable (not just highest revenue)
Missing and erroneous transaction data (items, locations, payment methods) creating blind spots
No forecasting capability to anticipate demand by product, day, or channel
Inability to compare in-store vs takeaway channel performance
No data-driven basis for menu pricing or promotional decisions
3. Goals & Success Metrics
3.1 Business Goals
Increase gross profit margin by 5–10% within 6 months of deployment through data-driven menu decisions
Reduce unclassified/error transaction rate from 9.6% to below 2% within 90 days
Enable management to make weekly menu and pricing decisions backed by data
Identify and act on revenue improvement opportunities worth at least £15,000 annually

4. Product Overview
4.1 System Architecture
The platform is composed of three layers:
Data Layer: Cleaned CSV ingestion pipeline with automated quality checks and imputation
ML Backend: Python (FastAPI) serving a Random Forest regression model for revenue prediction and an anomaly detector for data quality flagging
Frontend: React + TypeScript dashboard with Chart.js visualisations, filterable by date, item, location, and payment method

6.2 Critical Insights
Coffee and Tea have 70% margins but represent only 14% of total revenue — the single biggest profit improvement opportunity.
Salad is the top revenue item (£16,423) but carries only 45% margin. Supply cost renegotiation could add ~£800 profit annually per 1% margin gain.
Sandwich volume is strong (#2 by transactions) but 40% margin is the weakest. Premium variants (gourmet fillings, add-ons) could raise average ticket and margin.
Smoothies earn 55% margin with £12,860 revenue — an under-promoted star performer. Bundle deals and loyalty incentives would be high-ROI.
9.6% of revenue (£8,271) comes from transactions where the item is UNKNOWN or ERROR — a POS data entry problem that is masking true item-level performance.
39.6% of transactions are missing location data — the in-store vs takeaway split analysis is based on only 60% of data, making channel strategy decisions unreliable.

7.3 Model Parameters
Algorithm: RandomForestRegressor (scikit-learn)
n_estimators: 100 trees
max_depth: None (fully grown trees)
min_samples_split: 2
random_state: 42
Train/test split: 80% / 20% (stratified by month)
7.4 Model Outputs
Primary: Predicted total_spent per transaction
Secondary: Anomaly score (|actual - predicted| > 2 sigma flagged as data error)
Tertiary: Feature importance rankings for dashboard explainability
7.5 Retraining Strategy
Automated monthly retraining as new transaction data accumulates
Model drift monitoring: retrain if MAE exceeds £0.50 on validation set
A/B model versioning: new model must outperform current by >5% MAE to deploy

8.2 Medium Term (1–3 Months)
Introduce premium sandwich variants: add 2–3 gourmet options at £6–8 vs standard £4, targeting 20% of sandwich buyers to trade up. Projected impact: +£2,600–5,200 pa.
Loyalty programme for high-margin items: stamp card for Coffee and Tea drives frequency. A 10% increase in Coffee/Tea transactions adds ~£1,200 revenue at 70% margin.
Seasonal salad premium: Salad is already #1 revenue item — a seasonal premium version at £7–8 (vs standard £5) could lift average salad ticket by 25%, adding £4,100 pa.
Cookie bundle at checkout: Cookie is the lowest-revenue item but has 60% margin. A 'add a cookie for £1' prompt at checkout could increase attach rate significantly.
8.3 Long Term (3–6 Months)
Dynamic pricing pilot: raise Smoothie prices from £4 to £4.50 on peak weekend days. Demand elasticity is likely low for a treat item — potential £650 uplift with minimal volume loss.
Takeaway app integration: formalise the Takeaway channel with online pre-ordering. This also solves the 39.6% location-missing data problem automatically.
Review Cookie pricing: at £1/unit Cookie is very cheap. A 50p price increase to £1.50 (still cheap for a café) with only 10% volume elasticity adds £1,600 annual revenue.
